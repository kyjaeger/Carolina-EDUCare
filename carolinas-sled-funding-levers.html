The read for leadership

The four verticals do not pay for Cisco the same way, and treating them as one "education and healthcare" motion is what costs us deals. Universities are competitively grant-funded — we win by being written into a proposal 18 months before the PO. Community colleges run on formula money (Perkins) that is reliable, recurring, and explicitly barred from paying for capital work. K‑12 is the only vertical with a standing federal subsidy for the exact gear we sell, and in North Carolina the state pays the rest of it. Healthcare has almost no grant money for infrastructure — it is paid from operating margin, construction budgets, and a brand-new $50B rural program — and it buys on a 75% OpEx / 25% CapEx ratio that punishes capital-heavy proposals regardless of TCO.

Two structural facts should shape the fiscal-year plan. First, North Carolina is a materially better state than South Carolina for LAN and wireless selling in K‑12: the School Connectivity Initiative covers the non-E-Rate share of Category Two, making a district refresh close to net-zero locally, while SC's state match reached only 26 of 71 districts. Second, the two biggest healthcare tailwinds both have expiry dates attached — NC hospital margins are riding HASP directed payments that H.R.1 steps down 10% a year, and South Carolina's hospital Certificate of Need sunsets January 1, 2027, which has already started a construction race. FY2027 is a better year to close large healthcare capital in the Carolinas than FY2029.

What we do: lead K‑12 with Category Two internal connections where two price-aggressive competitors just lost their NC contract position; lead community colleges with Perkins refreshes where the curriculum is already Cisco-standardized; lead universities with the AI/HPC fabric where grant dollars and greenfield overlap; and lead healthcare with consolidation and enforcement — rationalizing security tooling and owning the segmentation plane that the proposed HIPAA Security Rule is about to make mandatory.

Biggest upside Healthcare, and it is new money

RHTP is the largest net-new funding event in either state. SC has already named 228 sub-grantees worth $167.3M — that is a published prospect list. NC's technology fund opens its first competitive round now.

Biggest risk The FCC is reviewing E-Rate itself

A June 2026 NPRM explicitly asks whether Category Two should continue at all. Nothing has changed operationally, but any C2 forecast past FY2028 needs a policy-risk discount — and a pull-forward argument.

Biggest self-inflicted wound Quoting dead SKUs into funded deals

What kills these deals
Grant timing is not our timing

NSF award schedules have been turbulent through 2025–26. Do not stake a committed forecast on a grant landing on schedule. Forecast the operating-budget motion; treat the grant-funded fabric as upside.

Open-source credibility

Duke and Clemson are both publicly shopping away from proprietary lock-in. A closed, proprietary-first fabric pitch loses on principle before it loses on price. Bring a SONiC-capable, RoCEv2-literate story or don't bring one.

Unconfirmed incumbents

Firewall, NAC, EDR and SIEM vendors are unconfirmed at several of these campuses. Treat them as first-call discovery questions, not assumptions, and re-verify before forecasting.

Vertical 02 · Community & technical colleges

Formula money is reliable and small; the state capital channel is where the size is

Three federal levers are viable: Perkins V (formula funds every college qualifies for now — the near-term sale), NSF ATE (competitive grants to build cyber-networking programs and equip their labs), and NSF CC* (the only one that funds production network and security gear, but it needs a research science driver most two-year colleges can't supply). Perkins leans our way through its industry-standard equipment test, but it bars capital work — cabling, electrical, construction — and it bars supplanting. South Carolina layers on two mechanisms North Carolina doesn't have in the same form: a centralized State Board equipment and capital ask that names IT explicitly, and readySC, which unlocks training-infrastructure money the moment an employer relocates.

What kills these deals
Uniform Guidance procurement

The moment federal dollars are involved, 2 CFR 200 competitive procurement applies. A sole-source Cisco award needs documented justification — standardization and interoperability are the defensible grounds. Current thresholds: micro-purchase $15,000, simplified acquisition $350,000 (effective Oct 1, 2025).

Price-reasonableness documentation

Since the 2024 Uniform Guidance revisions, recipients must keep documented evidence of price reasonableness even for micro-purchases when they don't solicit quotes. "Put it on the co-op contract" is no longer sufficient. Hand the customer the artifact proactively — contract discount schedule, published rate card, comparables.

CAE designations expire

Every cyber-defense designation runs a five-year cycle. Verify current status against the official CAE map rather than a dated press release — and treat each renewal as a buying trigger rather than a risk.

Perkins cannot pay for cabling

The most common scoping error in this vertical. Perkins funds the gear in the lab, not the pathway to it. Pair every Perkins lab deal with a capital source — a bond, the SBTCE equipment line, or the NC CTE-center capital priority — or the project stalls at the conduit.

What kills these deals
Claiming mandates that don't exist

Three false statements circulate constantly. The Joint Commission does not have new 2025/26 cybersecurity requirements — the 2026 standards are a streamlining release that eliminated 700+ elements of performance, and the Commission has formally argued against cyber Conditions of Participation. HIPAA does not yet require MFA or segmentation. CMS has not tied payment or CoPs to the HPH CPGs — they remain voluntary. Say "proposed."

The 340B and HASP squeeze lands in FY2027

The 340B rebate pilot starts January 1, 2027 and providers project substantial cash-flow drag, with at least one system publicly warning of "diversion of funding for capital." Combined with the 10%/year HASP step-down, expect DSH-heavy safety-net systems — ECU Health, MUSC, Prisma — to defer discretionary capital. Lead OpEx and as-a-service structures at those accounts specifically.

We do not know where we stand

Cisco's network and security incumbency is unconfirmed at every system in this vertical. That is normal — health systems don't publish infrastructure vendors — but it means install-base data has to come from CRM and partner records, not research. Do not let anyone treat the absence of evidence as evidence of absence, in either direction.

Every AI conversation needs a number

Bain and KLAS found the most common formal return threshold among healthcare buyers is 3.0x–3.9x return on original investment. Presenting AI-enablement infrastructure without a quantified operational return will not survive a CFO review, regardless of how good the architecture is.

Federal grant money Uniform Guidance 2 CFR 200 — current thresholds

Whenever Perkins, NSF, RHTP or any federal grant pays, 2 CFR 200.317–200.327 procurement standards attach, including to subrecipients.

Play 01
Get written into the proposal
Highest-leverage habit change available to this team

Grant money is committed 12–18 months before it is spent. By the time a PO appears the architecture was decided long ago, and it was decided by whoever helped the PI write the technical narrative.

The Cisco Public Funding Office (grantquestions@cisco.com) will supply grant verbiage for Cisco technology and review a draft proposal, with an advisor assigned per state. We are not using it. That is the single cheapest change on this list.

Play 02
Win the fabric on their terms
Where grant dollars and greenfield overlap

Duke is actively shopping merchant silicon and SONiC to escape lock-in. Clemson's own engineers have published a RoCEv2/Ethernet roadmap. These are sophisticated buyers with public positions.

Show up open-friendly and standards-first or we lose to Arista and NVIDIA InfiniBand on principle before we ever get to price. A closed, proprietary-first pitch is disqualifying in this room.

Play 03
Time security to the leader, not the quarter
Treat appointments as forecast events

NC State's CISO started June 2026. USC's CIO arrived in 2024. UNC has an undecided ZTNA bake-off with us already in the POC. Each of those is an open window that closes once the new leader's stack decisions harden.

Map every new CIO and CISO appointment in the territory and treat it as a forecast event on the same footing as a budget cycle. Nobody on the team is currently tracking this systematically.

Risk
Grant timing is not our timing
Forecast discipline

NSF award schedules have been turbulent through 2025–26. Do not stake a committed forecast on a grant landing on schedule.

The right structure is to forecast the operating-budget motion and treat the grant-funded fabric as upside. Teams that invert this will miss quarters for reasons entirely outside their control.

Risk
Open-source credibility
Disqualification risk, not price risk

Duke and Clemson are both publicly shopping away from proprietary lock-in. A closed, proprietary-first fabric pitch loses on principle before it loses on price — and it damages the relationship for the next cycle.

Bring a SONiC-capable, RoCEv2-literate story into these accounts or do not bring one at all.

Risk
Unconfirmed incumbents
Discovery gap

Firewall, NAC, EDR and SIEM vendors are unconfirmed at several of these campuses. Universities do not publish their infrastructure vendors and research will not close the gap.

Treat these as first-call discovery questions rather than assumptions, and re-verify before anything goes into forecast.

NCCCS
NC equipment allotment
$48,962,762 recurring · all 58 colleges · FY2026-27 enacted
Why this matters
North Carolina pays for instructional and administrative equipment as a matter of statute. G.S. 115D-31(a)(1) makes “furniture and equipment for administrative and instructional purposes” the State’s obligation, funded through this recurring allotment under purpose code 920, with items over $10,000 capitalised. G.S. 115D-32(a)(1) leaves counties the buildings, vehicles, grounds and plant equipment — and anything not for instructional or administrative use.
Funds
A recurring statewide allotment, flat against FY2025-26, distributed to all 58 colleges. On top of it sits $14.06M of directed capital and equipment grants to named colleges, the largest being GTCC’s aviation centre at $7.5M — note a technical corrections bill cut that from the $9.623M still circulating in State Board material.
Won't fund
Structured cabling, pathways and anything delivered as building infrastructure — that is county plant-fund money, which is why Wake Tech’s bond names technology separately. Also note G.S. 143C-8-7.1(e): state capital funds cannot buy building systems unless the cost is recovered in 10 years through reduced operating costs, attested at design development. That is a hard gate on smart-building and energy-management pitches.
Cisco
This is the quiet annuity in the vertical: recurring, formula, no competition for the appropriation, and statutorily aimed at exactly the instructional network gear we sell. There is no NC SCIF money for community colleges at all this year — the $400M program from 2021 ran out and has no successor — so do not wait for state capital here. Work the allotment and the county bonds.

Play 01
Lead with Perkins everywhere
Available at every college, every year

Perkins is the only lever in this vertical that is always on. And the industry-standard equipment test does our sole-source justification work for us wherever the curriculum already runs on Cisco Networking Academy.

Central Piedmont, Greenville Tech and Spartanburg CC are all built that way today. The curriculum decision made years ago is the procurement argument now.

Play 02
Sell to the consortium, not the campus
Leverage is the only way this vertical pays

Fayetteville Tech co-founds and leads the 18-college Carolina Cyber Network. Clemson operates SC-REN. Standardization decisions made at consortium level scale across dozens of institutions for the same selling effort.

Deal size per campus is genuinely small here. If we run this vertical as 74 individual account plans the math never works — the coverage cost exceeds the revenue.

Play 03
Treat designations and relocations as triggers
Build a trigger calendar, not a call cadence

Four events unlock a lab refresh: an expiring NSA cyber-defense designation, a fresh CAE award, a passing bond, and a readySC-anchored employer landing.

None of those follow our fiscal calendar. Build a trigger calendar and work it, rather than running a uniform call cadence across 74 colleges that mostly have no money this quarter.

Risk
Perkins cannot pay for cabling
The most common scoping error in this vertical

Perkins funds the gear in the lab, not the pathway to it. Projects scoped without this in mind stall at the conduit — the switches arrive and there is no money to connect them.

Pair every Perkins lab deal with a capital source: a county bond, the SBTCE equipment line, or the NC CTE-center capital priority. If there isn't one, the deal is not real yet.

Risk
Uniform Guidance procurement
2 CFR 200 · applies the moment federal dollars appear

A sole-source Cisco award needs documented justification. Standardization and interoperability are the defensible grounds, but they have to be written down before the award, not reconstructed afterward.

Current thresholds: micro-purchase $15,000, simplified acquisition $350,000, effective October 1, 2025.

Risk
Price-reasonableness documentation
Changed in the 2024 Uniform Guidance revisions

Recipients must now keep documented evidence of price reasonableness even for micro-purchases when they don't solicit quotes. "Put it on the co-op contract" is no longer sufficient on its own.

Hand the customer the artifact proactively — contract discount schedule, published rate card, comparables. It costs us nothing and it removes the most common reason a funded deal sits unsigned.

Risk
CAE designations expire
Five-year cycle · verify, don't assume

Every NSA cyber-defense designation runs a five-year cycle. Verify current status against the official CAE map rather than a dated press release — several in-territory claims trace back to announcements that are now stale.

Handled correctly this is an opportunity, not just a hygiene item: treat each renewal as a buying trigger.

Play 01
Open with a C2 budget utilization review
Legitimate, urgent, and not a sales pitch

Category Two budgets are five-year and non-rolling. A district that underspends permanently forfeits the money — there is no carryforward and no appeal.

Student counts are validated the first year a district applies in the cycle and then locked for five years, and enrollment is falling across both states. Districts that haven't yet validated their FY2026–2030 count have a shrinking window to lock in a larger number. That conversation is genuinely in the district's interest, which is why it opens doors that a product pitch doesn't.

Play 02
Work the contract structure, not the list price
Two price-aggressive competitors just lost position

NCDPI dropped Fortinet and Ubiquiti from the Category Two master contract for the FY26 term, and Palo Alto can only bid firewalls and managed services on that vehicle. Cisco and Meraki now cover five of nine segments — broader than anyone except Juniper.

Every deal still runs through a price-weighted mini-bid, so we win on the permitted secondary factors: compatibility with existing infrastructure, prior performance, references and training. Those are winnable and we under-invest in documenting them.

Play 03

Lever
Certificate of Need — and the IT exemption
State regulatory · NC and SC diverge
The exemption that matters
In North Carolina, network and data-processing infrastructure is CON-exempt (N.C.G.S. 131E‑184(a)(3)). Every other project in a health system’s pipeline takes six to fourteen months of state review. Network spend does not. Duke has already used this on a networking project at Lake Norman.
Why that is the opening
It means network modernisation can proceed on its own timeline, decoupled from the regulatory queue. It is the one capital conversation in NC healthcare that nobody has to wait for.
South Carolina
CON sunsets January 1, 2027. Expect a construction surge as projects that were previously blocked become filable. That is a pipeline signal for Prisma, MUSC and Roper — none of which are on our FY27 list.
Two cautions on the exemption
First, the statute exempts “data processing equipment” — it does not use the word network. The practical effect is the same, but quote the statutory language, not a paraphrase. Second, the sweeping CON exemption written for NC Children’s was dropped from the enacted budget. It existed as S257 Edition 2 §5.6 and would have exempted equipment “regardless of cost,” but it is not law. That project currently has no CON, no exemption and no need determination.

Lever
FCC Rural Health Care / Healthcare Connect Fund
Federal · standing subsidy · 65% flat
What it pays
A flat 65% of eligible connectivity costs for eligible rural health care providers (47 CFR § 54.607), with bed-count caps.
What it will not pay
Computers, end-user wireless devices, tablets and video conferencing equipment are explicitly ineligible. This is the most commonly oversold lever in healthcare — it is a circuit and connectivity subsidy, not an equipment subsidy.
Where it fits
Rural affiliate connectivity and WAN, which in practice means it supports the transport layer under a telehealth or hub-and-spoke build rather than the gear at either end.

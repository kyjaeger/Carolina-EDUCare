Where the Money Comes From

Area Sales Director — Shawn Gavin Area SE Director — Vinaya Springer September 2026 · 5-minute view

Four verticals in North and South Carolina. Each one pays for Cisco a completely different way — competitive grants, federal formula money, a standing federal subsidy, and operating margin. Treating them as one motion is what costs us deals. Pick a vertical to go a level deeper; click any lever, opportunity or risk for the detail behind it.

Money follows the science driver. The AI/HPC fabric is the one place where grant dollars, greenfield construction and our strongest silicon story land in the same room — and it is also where Arista and NVIDIA are strongest, so we earn it on merit.

RCI
NSF Campus Cyberinfrastructure (CC*) & MRI
$0.5–4M each · competitive · annual solicitation
Funds
Production campus networking, Science DMZ, data-transfer nodes, GPU-as-a-service clusters and research interconnect. This is the only federal education lever that pays for real production network and security gear.
Won't fund
Anything without a credible research "science driver." Enterprise IT, administrative networks and teaching labs do not qualify.
Cisco
Nexus 9000 / Silicon One, Nexus Hyperfabric AI, UCS and AI PODs, ThousandEyes across the Science DMZ. Speak RoCEv2, not "AI-ready" — these buyers are researchers.
RCI

$39M–$160M · live in territory today
Funds
Mission-specific compute and secure enclaves. In territory right now: NC State leads the DoD CLAWS microelectronics hub at $39.4M; UNC Charlotte leads an NSF Regional Innovation Engine worth up to $160M over ten years; Duke's ~$40M GPU data center is grant- and philanthropy-funded.
Won't fund
A campus-wide refresh. Scope stays strictly inside the funded program's boundary.
Cisco
Secure OT/IoT for the semiconductor and grid partners, Cyber Vision (included with IE3500/IE9300 plus Network Advantage), and ThousandEyes across multi-partner ecosystems where no one owns the whole path.

Play 01
Get written into the proposal
Highest-leverage habit change available to this team

Grant money is committed 12–18 months before it is spent. By the time a PO appears the architecture was decided long ago, and it was decided by whoever helped the PI write the technical narrative.

The Cisco Public Funding Office (grantquestions@cisco.com) will supply grant verbiage for Cisco technology and review a draft proposal, with an advisor assigned per state. We are not using it. That is the single cheapest change on this list.

Play 02
Win the fabric on their terms
Where grant dollars and greenfield overlap

Duke is actively shopping merchant silicon and SONiC to escape lock-in. Clemson's own engineers have published a RoCEv2/Ethernet roadmap. These are sophisticated buyers with public positions.

Show up open-friendly and standards-first or we lose to Arista and NVIDIA InfiniBand on principle before we ever get to price. A closed, proprietary-first pitch is disqualifying in this room.

Play 03
Time security to the leader, not the quarter
Treat appointments as forecast events

NC State's CISO started June 2026. USC's CIO arrived in 2024. UNC has an undecided ZTNA bake-off with us already in the POC. Each of those is an open window that closes once the new leader's stack decisions harden.

Map every new CIO and CISO appointment in the territory and treat it as a forecast event on the same footing as a budget cycle. Nobody on the team is currently tracking this systematically.

Risk
Grant timing is not our timing
Forecast discipline

NSF award schedules have been turbulent through 2025–26. Do not stake a committed forecast on a grant landing on schedule.

The right structure is to forecast the operating-budget motion and treat the grant-funded fabric as upside. Teams that invert this will miss quarters for reasons entirely outside their control.

Risk
Open-source credibility
Disqualification risk, not price risk

Duke and Clemson are both publicly shopping away from proprietary lock-in. A closed, proprietary-first fabric pitch loses on principle before it loses on price — and it damages the relationship for the next cycle.

Bring a SONiC-capable, RoCEv2-literate story into these accounts or do not bring one at all.

Risk
Unconfirmed incumbents
Discovery gap

Firewall, NAC, EDR and SIEM vendors are unconfirmed at several of these campuses. Universities do not publish their infrastructure vendors and research will not close the gap.

Treat these as first-call discovery questions rather than assumptions, and re-verify before anything goes into forecast.

Perkins V
Strengthening CTE for the 21st Century Act
Formula · every one of the 74 colleges qualifies today
Funds
Instructional and lab equipment tied to a CTE program. Switches, routers, firewalls and access points for a teaching lab are a clean fit, and the industry-standard equipment test actively favors a vendor the curriculum already names.
Won't fund
Capital work — cabling, electrical, construction — or the campus backbone. It also cannot supplant existing spend, which rules out swapping it in for something already budgeted.
Cisco
Lab-grade Catalyst, Meraki, Secure Firewall, ISE tied to CCNA and CyberOps tracks. Networking Academy curriculum is the written basis for the industry-standard justification — it is a procurement document, not a marketing asset.
NSF ATE
Advanced Technological Education
Competitive · gated on grant track record
Funds
Launching or expanding two-year technician programs and equipping their labs. Larger and considerably more flexible than Perkins.
Won't fund
A college with no grant history. Track record is the real gate — which is why Wake Tech and Fayetteville Tech are credible candidates and Midlands and Horry-Georgetown are not yet.
Cisco
Same lab stack at bigger scope, plus the Public Funding Office will supply grant verbiage and review the proposal. Helping a college win its first ATE award is the highest-trust thing we can do anywhere in this vertical.
NSF CC*
Campus Cyberinfrastructure
$0.5–1M · requires a research science driver
Funds
Real production networking and security infrastructure. It is the only lever in this vertical that does.
Won't fund
Anything without a research science driver. For most two-year colleges that means partnering with a university or an industry research anchor, or not pursuing it at all.
Cisco
Production Catalyst / Nexus, Secure Firewall, ISE, ThousandEyes. Best near-term shots are Wake Tech (RTP and biotech adjacency) and Greenville Tech once its Industrial Cybersecurity and AI Center is operating.

NC CAP

Funds
Construction and renovation — the only way to pay for the cabling and electrical that Perkins will not touch. A ~$149M Wake Tech GO bond is on the November 3, 2026 ballot, and the NC Needs-Based Public School Capital Fund now prioritizes CTE Centers serving an entire public school unit, which is technology-dense by definition.
Won't fund

Play 01
Lead with Perkins everywhere
Available at every college, every year

Perkins is the only lever in this vertical that is always on. And the industry-standard equipment test does our sole-source justification work for us wherever the curriculum already runs on Cisco Networking Academy.

Central Piedmont, Greenville Tech and Spartanburg CC are all built that way today. The curriculum decision made years ago is the procurement argument now.

Play 02
Sell to the consortium, not the campus
Leverage is the only way this vertical pays

Fayetteville Tech co-founds and leads the 18-college Carolina Cyber Network. Clemson operates SC-REN. Standardization decisions made at consortium level scale across dozens of institutions for the same selling effort.

Deal size per campus is genuinely small here. If we run this vertical as 74 individual account plans the math never works — the coverage cost exceeds the revenue.

Play 03
Treat designations and relocations as triggers
Build a trigger calendar, not a call cadence

Four events unlock a lab refresh: an expiring NSA cyber-defense designation, a fresh CAE award, a passing bond, and a readySC-anchored employer landing.

None of those follow our fiscal calendar. Build a trigger calendar and work it, rather than running a uniform call cadence across 74 colleges that mostly have no money this quarter.

Risk
Perkins cannot pay for cabling
The most common scoping error in this vertical

Perkins funds the gear in the lab, not the pathway to it. Projects scoped without this in mind stall at the conduit — the switches arrive and there is no money to connect them.

Pair every Perkins lab deal with a capital source: a county bond, the SBTCE equipment line, or the NC CTE-center capital priority. If there isn't one, the deal is not real yet.

Risk
Uniform Guidance procurement
2 CFR 200 · applies the moment federal dollars appear

A sole-source Cisco award needs documented justification. Standardization and interoperability are the defensible grounds, but they have to be written down before the award, not reconstructed afterward.

Current thresholds: micro-purchase $15,000, simplified acquisition $350,000, effective October 1, 2025.

Risk
Price-reasonableness documentation
Changed in the 2024 Uniform Guidance revisions

Recipients must now keep documented evidence of price reasonableness even for micro-purchases when they don't solicit quotes. "Put it on the co-op contract" is no longer sufficient on its own.

Hand the customer the artifact proactively — contract discount schedule, published rate card, comparables. It costs us nothing and it removes the most common reason a funded deal sits unsigned.

Risk
CAE designations expire
Five-year cycle · verify, don't assume

Every NSA cyber-defense designation runs a five-year cycle. Verify current status against the official CAE map rather than a dated press release — several in-territory claims trace back to announcements that are now stale.

Handled correctly this is an opportunity, not just a hygiene item: treat each renewal as a buying trigger.

E-Rate C2
Category Two internal connections
$201.57/student · FY2026–FY2030 cycle · non-rolling
Funds
Switches, routers, access points, wireless controllers, cabling, racks, UPS, caching and basic firewall, plus the software that supports them including bug fixes and security patches. Discounts run to 85%. Budget is the greater of $201.57 × students or $30,175 × schools.
Won't fund
Advanced / next-gen firewalls — the FCC declined again for FY2026. Also excluded: anti-virus, anti-spam, IDS/IPS, content filtering, end-user devices, spares and redundant failover components, and 24-hour network monitoring. Budgets do not roll over between cycles.
Cisco
Multi-year Meraki term licenses are listed at 100% eligible, and the FY2026 reclassification of service SKUs to Internal Connections means they can be requested in multi-year terms and paid in full in year one. That is the single most important commercial mechanic in this vertical. Verify every SKU at ciscoerate.com first.
Cyber Pilot
FCC Schools & Libraries Cybersecurity Pilot
$200M over 3 years · all application windows closed
Funds
The four things E‑Rate won't: advanced/next-gen firewalls and FWaaS (HA pairs included), endpoint protection, identity and MFA, and monitoring, detection and response — plus DNS-layer security, NAC, SASE, logging, email and web security, and immutable backups.
Won't fund
New applicants. 707 participants were selected in January 2025 and all windows are closed; roughly $49.8M is committed against $3.7B of expressed demand. Basic firewalls are categorically excluded even if the applicant forgoes E‑Rate. No second window has been announced.
Cisco
The 13 NC and 10 SC named participants are a qualified, funded prospect list — the best one in this entire briefing. Map Secure Firewall, Secure Endpoint, Secure Access, Duo, ISE, XDR, Splunk to the four categories. Re-map away from Umbrella and Meraki Systems Manager — both appear in our own solution guide and both are end-of-sale.
NC SCI
NC School Connectivity Initiative
$12.7M state C2 support · North Carolina only
Funds
The non-discount share of Category Two, allotted back to the district through PRC 073. In SFY2024 the state put $12.71M behind $57.28M of C2 spend. A C2 refresh in North Carolina costs the district essentially nothing out of local funds — and most reps do not know this.

NCDPI's own report to the General Assembly flags advanced firewall and advanced endpoint protection as underfunded since COVID money expired — their words, and exactly what the Cyber Pilot funds.

SC CAP
SC penny tax, bonds & K‑12 Tech Initiative
~$91M IT earmark on the Nov 3, 2026 ballot
Funds
The 1% Education Capital Improvement Tax is the real South Carolina money. Charleston County's Nov 3 extension projects ~$1.25B including ~$91M explicitly for information and technology updates and ~$15M for security — and this tax has passed every time it has appeared since 2010. Cisco
Full estate, but the financial pitch has to lean on the district's own capital and sales-tax revenue rather than a state match. Expect harder price competition in SC than in NC and plan partner strategy accordingly.
SAFETY
School safety & physical security
$20M SC · $32.9M NC SRO · carryforward to Jun 30, 2027
Funds
Cameras, access control, mass notification, two-way radio and door locking. SC allocates $20M with "electronic or other technology" as an eligible category. NC's SRO money is recurring and flowing ($32.9M to 198 units), and districts holding 2021-23 and 2023-25 School Safety Grant balances have until June 30, 2027 to spend them — live, already-appropriated money sitting unspent. Cisco
Meraki MV cameras and MT sensors on a cloud-managed estate, Cisco Spaces, and Webex for notification workflows. NC statute requires updating law-enforcement documentation whenever access control devices change — a built-in reason to get the district and the sheriff in the same conversation.
Dead
ESSER · bus Wi‑Fi · hotspots · Digital Equity
Confirmed closed — scrub from every forecast
Status
ESSER's final liquidation deadline passed March 28, 2026 and the Department confirmed no further extensions. Nothing replaced it. School bus Wi‑Fi and off-premises hotspots became ineligible September 30, 2025, reversing the 2023–24 expansions. The Digital Equity Act state capacity program remains canceled.
Action
Push back on any forecast that still lines up ESSER. There is a use for this, though: if a district's technology plan still references bus Wi‑Fi or hotspots as E‑Rate-funded, that plan predates September 2025 — which is a credible, entirely non-salesy reason to request a planning conversation.
Play 01
Open with a C2 budget utilization review
Legitimate, urgent, and not a sales pitch

Category Two budgets are five-year and non-rolling. A district that underspends permanently forfeits the money — there is no carryforward and no appeal.

Student counts are validated the first year a district applies in the cycle and then locked for five years, and enrollment is falling across both states. Districts that haven't yet validated their FY2026–2030 count have a shrinking window to lock in a larger number. That conversation is genuinely in the district's interest, which is why it opens doors that a product pitch doesn't.

Play 02
Work the contract structure, not the list price
Two price-aggressive competitors just lost position

NCDPI dropped Fortinet and Ubiquiti from the Category Two master contract for the FY26 term, and Palo Alto can only bid firewalls and managed services on that vehicle. Cisco and Meraki now cover five of nine segments — broader than anyone except Juniper.

Every deal still runs through a price-weighted mini-bid, so we win on the permitted secondary factors: compatibility with existing infrastructure, prior performance, references and training. Those are winnable and we under-invest in documenting them.

Play 03
Fix the partner math in North Carolina
Structural disadvantage in the mini-bid mechanic

CDW-G carries nearly every competitor on the NCDPI contract but not Cisco — and was the top E‑Rate service provider by commitment in a recent FY2026 decision wave.

In any NC mini-bid they are quoting three or four competitors while our quote comes from a narrower partner set. That is not a selling-skill problem, it is a structural one, and it should shape partner strategy deliberately rather than deal by deal.

Risk
The FCC is reviewing Category Two itself
June 2026 NPRM · the biggest policy risk in the briefing

Chairman Carr's "top-to-bottom review" — the most comprehensive since 1997 — explicitly asks whether internal connections funding should continue at all. Comments closed October 13; replies are due November 12.

Nothing has changed operationally and FY2026 was fully funded. But discount any C2 forecast past FY2028. The flip side is that this is a legitimate pull-forward argument with districts sitting on unspent budget.

Risk
The SC "compatible solutions" lot structure
Every SC installed base is now contested ground

South Carolina's new five-year Category Two statewide contract gives Cisco its own lot, which reads like a win. But every lot permits "functionally equivalent solutions fully compatible with the end user's existing environment."

That means a competitor can bid Cisco-compatible gear into our lot, against our own installed base. Confirm the September 8 award results immediately and brief the SC team on what the language actually allows.

Risk
Competing against free in North Carolina
Positioning error, entirely self-inflicted

NCDIT gives districts managed firewall, content filtering, endpoint protection and IAM at no charge, and MCNC performs network and security assessments free.

Leading with perimeter security or a paid assessment in NC loses to free — and it makes us look like we didn't do our homework. Lead with C2 internal connections and in-LAN segmentation instead, where the shared services don't reach.

Risk
Our own end-of-life SKUs
Biggest self-inflicted wound in the whole briefing

Umbrella goes end-of-sale January 31, 2027. Meraki Systems Manager already did, on June 3, 2026. Both are still named in Cisco's own K‑12 Cybersecurity Pilot solution guide.

Re-map to Secure Access / DNS Defense and Ivanti Neurons for MDM before the next quote goes out. Quoting a dead SKU into a funded, competitively-reviewed deal is the kind of error that costs the relationship, not just the order.

RHTP
Rural Health Transformation Program (CMS)
NC $213.0M · SC $200.0M year one · FY2027 notice Oct 31
Funds
$50B over FY2026–FY2030. The statutory uses name remote monitoring, robotics, artificial intelligence, software and hardware for significant IT advances, and cybersecurity capability development outright — unusually explicit language for a health program. SC has already awarded 228 sub-grants worth $167.3M, which is a published, named prospect list. NC runs a Rural Health Innovation Fund of up to $20M/yr with the State Treasurer helping providers assess systems, vet vendors and manage procurement.
Won't fund
Growth on top of a stable baseline. NC's own Governor's office paired the award with a warning about ~$50B in Medicaid federal funds lost over ten years. Half the annual allocation is performance-recalculated, so out-year money is not guaranteed.
Cisco
Read the NC and SC state project abstracts before any rural conversation and build the motion around what each state actually committed to. CMS assigns a state project officer, so there is a known owner to map.
HIPAA
Proposed HIPAA Security Rule overhaul
~$9B industry cost year one · final action anticipated ~Jul 2027
Funds
Nothing directly — but it creates board-level obligation, which is better than a grant. The NPRM would mandate multi-factor authentication, network segmentation, an annually refreshed technology asset inventory and a network map showing how ePHI moves, encryption at rest and in transit, six-month vulnerability scans, annual penetration testing, and 72-hour restoration procedures. It eliminates the "addressable" loophole that has let systems defer for twenty years.
Won't fund
Anything today. It is pending and has moved to Long-Term Actions with anticipated final action July 2027. Say "proposed," always — a CISO will correct you and you will lose the room.
Cisco
The four load-bearing items map directly: MFA → Duo; segmentation → ISE, SD-Access, Hypershield; asset inventory and ePHI network map → Medical NAC + Claroty, ThousandEyes, Splunk. All are multi-quarter programs. Systems that start in FY2027 will be ready; systems that wait for the final rule will not.
SC CON

Cisco

NC HASP
NC directed payments & the margin window
~$3.4B/yr · steps down 10% per year under H.R.1
Funds
Hospital operating margin, which is what actually pays for infrastructure in this vertical. Both our anchor accounts are carried by it. UNC Health recognized $416M in FY2025 — strip it out and their 7.2% operating margin falls to roughly 1.6%. Duke Health recognized $417.2M gross, $269.7M net, against total operating income of $179.0M, meaning their FY2025 operations were negative without it. Novant posted $1.16B net income; Atrium $1.66B+.
Won't fund
Indefinitely — but note the timing carefully, because getting it wrong cuts against us. The 10%/year step-down has not started. It begins with rating periods on or after January 1, 2028, running until payments are capped at 100% of Medicare, and the provider-tax safe harbor separately drops to 3.5% by 2032. The NC Healthcare Association's read is that HASP's benefit is then "virtually eliminated" — up to $6.5B for North Carolina. FY2025 was the largest HASP year on record at both anchors. The pressure is anticipated, not yet realized.
Cisco
That timing is the argument, not a caveat. Boards are pulling capital forward while balance sheets are strong and will tighten once the step-down bites. UNC Health filing roughly $1.77B of new-hospital CONs inside ten weeks, while HASP is at peak and credit outlooks are positive, is exactly that behavior. FY2027 is a better year to close large NC healthcare capital than FY2029. Say it to the account teams explicitly.
FCC RHC
FCC Rural Health Care / Healthcare Connect Fund
$744.2M program cap · flat 65% · FY27 window opens Dec 1
Funds
A flat 65% discount on broadband and network services for rural healthcare providers and consortia, plus network equipment necessary to make the funded HCF broadband functional. Consortium applicants can get upfront charges and multi-year commitments.
Won't fund
A wireless refresh. Computers, end-user wireless devices, tablets, smartphones and video/audio/web conferencing equipment are explicitly ineligible, and equipment support is unavailable for networks not dedicated to healthcare. The 35% match cannot come from the vendor — that rule trips up vendor-financed deals more than any other in the program.
Cisco
Treat it as a consortium WAN/SD-WAN and dedicated-network play, not a campus play. Best fit is a multi-site rural system that already has an RHC consultant engaged.
AHCAH
Hospital-at-home & telehealth certainty
Authorized through Sep 30, 2030 · five-year planning horizon
Funds
Nothing directly — but it is the most useful regulatory fact in this vertical for a networking seller. Cisco

Small
USDA DLT · HRSA SHIP · state capital
$13.5K–$750K · tactical only, do not forecast
Funds
USDA DLT: $50K–$750K awards with a 15% non-federal match and unusually favorable eligibility — AV equipment, monitors and displays, telemedical devices, cybersecurity software and videoconferencing licenses tied to the project. HRSA SHIP: ~$13.5K per hospital under 50 beds, with cybersecurity software subscriptions, training and risk assessments explicitly allowable. NC state capital has also delivered $40M to ECU Health / Martin County and $208.5M in health capital for the biennium.
Won't fund
Infrastructure at scale. DLT caps applicant-owned broadband facilities at 20% of the grant and is currently not accepting applications due to funding constraints. SHIP is a subscription-renewal budget, not a project budget. Do not build a forecast on either.
Cisco
DLT is the telehealth-room program lever and it is the mirror image of FCC RHC — it funds endpoints and video where RHC won't, and caps the network where RHC pays. A rural hospital can reasonably run both at once, which is a useful thing to know in the room.
Anchor #1
Duke Health
$7.29B FY2025 · four hospitals, 1,821 beds · 38,000+ employees
How they buy
Private nonprofit controlled by Duke University — they cannot reliably buy off NC state term contracts. Real vehicles are Vizient (signed jointly for the health system and the university) and direct negotiation; Vizient's own CFO described Duke's prior posture as "a self-contracting approach." DHTS and Duke University OIT are separate buying centers with separate security reviews — a contract with one does not cover the other.
Who owns it
There is no CIO of Duke Health. Randy Arvay is CTO and CISO, running ~600 people across hybrid infrastructure, cloud, unified communications and cyber defense. One person owns the network budget and the security budget. Jeffrey Ferranti is Chief Digital Officer, not CIO — he left that role in January 2022. Route medical-device security through Eric Poon, MD (CHIO); Clinical Engineering and its 50,000+ devices report to him.
The money
Operating margin swung from 0.3% to 4.6% in the first half of FY2026 — $189.8M of operating income against $12.0M the prior-year half. But HASP was $417.2M gross and $269.7M net in FY2025, against total operating income of $179.0M. Without HASP their operations were negative. Capital in flight: Cary $854.2M, Garner ED $32.3M, and two bed projects at $39.9M and $23M. Rated Aa3 / AA− / AA−, with $540M of bonds cleared in June 2025 explicitly including "equipping a new facility."
The opening
Network and IT infrastructure is Certificate-of-Need exempt in North Carolina, and Duke has already used the exemption on a networking project — their counsel filed under the data-processing-equipment provision to upgrade networking and wireless at Lake Norman even though the cost cleared the CON threshold. Every other Duke project took 6–14 months of state review. Network spend does not. It is the fastest-closing category in their capital stack. Their 2018–19 fiber core was sold internally as a seven-year investment, which puts it at refresh age now.
Watch out
Duke is four hospitals, not three — they bought Lake Norman Regional in Mooresville for $284.3M in April 2025, their first hospital outside the Triangle and their entry into the Charlotte metro. Do not say "Private Diagnostic Clinic"; it was folded into Duke Health Integrated Practice. Their ABCDS Oversight program governs every algorithmic solution touching patient care, including procured ones — budget for that gate on anything with embedded analytics. And we have no verified network incumbency at Duke Health in either direction; a search tool claimed Cisco NAC and the cited source does not say that.
Anchor #2
UNC Health
$7.40B FY2025 · ~16 hospitals, ~20 campuses · all 100 counties
How they buy
The most useful fact in this briefing. UNC Health is a State agency and political subdivision — a federal court called it an "arm of the State" — and it is expressly exempt from North Carolina's core purchasing statutes under G.S. 116-350.45. Its own Board sets purchasing policy; the Division of Purchase and Contract gets review and comment only. State term contracts are a pricing option, never a mandate. It also holds its own bond authority and self-governs design and construction vendor selection. Do not sell UNC Health as a "state contract" account, and do not sell it as a private nonprofit either — neither shorthand survives contact with the statute.
Who owns it
Brent Lamm, SVP and CIO since July 2022 and inside UNC Health for 14+ years — an internal promotion, not an outside hire. Dee Young is CISO (formerly HCA), and S. David McSwain, MD is System CMIO and the clinical gatekeeper for enterprise technology standardization. Clinical engineering reports up through IT, so medical-device security is an ISD budget line rather than a facilities one. New CEO Dr. Cristy Page took office November 2025 and her own specialty is rural health and primary care.
The structural gift
Information Services Division is centralized, and new hospitals get no local IT organization. UNC Health's own Asheville CON filing states that information technology "will be provided via centralized UNC Health Shared Services." That means network, security and infrastructure decisions for every new facility are made once, in Chapel Hill. Sell one enterprise agreement to ISD rather than running a campaign per site. Their vendor contracts are also shielded from public records under G.S. 116-350.65 — we cannot FOIA the incumbent, and competitors cannot FOIA us.
The money
FY2025 operating revenue $7.404B on a 7.2% margin — but $416M of that was HASP. Strip HASP out and the margin is roughly 1.6%. Roughly $1.77B of net-new hospital CONs are awaiting decision right now: Wilmington $852.7M for 150 beds, Asheville $747.4M for 92 beds, and 233 beds at Rex for $169.6M with a hearing October 15. The Asheville filing publishes its technology lines — $31.5M of non-medical equipment and $8.2M of consultant fees covering low-voltage systems and commissioning. They have issued no new-money bonds since February 2021, so the financing conversation is still open.
Funding levers unique to them
UNC Health is the NC ROOTS Hub Lead for Region 4 — the only provider organization holding a hub lead in the entire $213M/year Rural Health Transformation Program. It governs and sub-grants across 14 counties, and the program has a named digital-health workstream. Separately, $40M of NC CARE money was announced for UNC Health Rockingham in February 2026 explicitly to "enhance critical infrastructure." Note the limit: outside Region 4 their own affiliates sit under other hub leads, so a single "UNC Health RHTP" motion will not work.
Watch out
The Cisco Meraki wireless selection everyone is citing belongs to UNC‑Chapel Hill's ITS — the university, not the health system. They are separate organizations with separate procurement authority. Walking into UNC Health claiming a Meraki win would be immediately and visibly wrong. Also: the Cary campus is not imminent — approved services date is January 2032 — and it is physically in Durham County despite the name.
The rest

Advocate / Atrium Health — Charlotte, over $38.9B revenue and the largest opportunity in the region. Atrium planned $1.07B of capital in 2026, and the WakeMed combination (a $2B investment pledge, 3,300 jobs) cleared its county vote in September 2026. The integration architecture is being decided in Q4 2026 and H1 2027.

Prisma Health — a $500M commercial paper program across five named projects. MUSC — a $1.115B cancer hospital with a $230M equipment and furnishings line, and the one system in the territory where a single enterprise CIO covers both the university and the health system. Novant — $11.91B revenue, simultaneously defending Wilmington against UNC Health, entering Asheville, and entering South Carolina.

Roper St. Francis — $1.2B and 805,000 sq ft in North Charleston, steel going up now for a 2029 opening, delivered under a target-value method where early technical engagement disproportionately shapes outcomes. ECU Health — DSH-heavy and squeezed; lead OpEx there specifically.

The pattern worth naming to leadership: out-of-state systems entering South Carolina will extend existing enterprise standards from Charlotte, Winston-Salem and Gastonia. The incumbent-vendor decision happens at the parent, not the site — so follow the parent relationship rather than the geography.

Play 01
Lead with consolidation, not capability
Ride the dominant budget motion instead of fighting it

CHIME's 2026 CIO survey puts application and portfolio rationalization tied with cybersecurity and EHR optimization at 22% each as the top budget priority, with 61% calling rationalization high-urgency. Systems are explicitly funding AI by retiring applications.

A proposal that adds a platform is fighting for share of a contested budget. One that replaces three or four tools is riding the motion the CIO is already being measured on. Same technology, completely different reception.

Play 02
Structure it as OpEx or lose on structure
75% OpEx / 25% CapEx — not a preference, a constraint

70% of health-system CIOs report a 75% OpEx / 25% CapEx split, essentially unchanged year over year, even as a third now allocate 5% of net patient revenue to IT. Subscription and consumption models match how the money actually moves.

A capital-heavy proposal loses here regardless of how the TCO math works. And managed SOC is the single function CIOs most want to outsource at 22% — the highest-intent adjacency anywhere in this vertical.

Play 03
Win the enforcement plane, don't fight the discovery vendors
A much better fight, and the NPRM makes it mandatory

Claroty has taken Best in KLAS for healthcare IoT security four years running. Armis, Asimily, Forescout and Palo Alto are all Leaders. We are a Strong Contender with a huge network footprint and less clinical workflow depth — that is an honest read of where we sit.

Where discovery is already deployed, the question is not "how do we displace them" — it is "who enforces the segmentation policy their discovery recommends." We win that one on network position, and the proposed HIPAA rule is about to make it a requirement rather than a project.

Risk
Claiming mandates that don't exist
Credibility risk — these buyers will catch it

Three false statements circulate constantly in this market. The Joint Commission does not have new 2025/26 cybersecurity requirements — the 2026 standards are a streamlining release that eliminated 700+ elements of performance, and the Commission has formally argued against cyber Conditions of Participation. HIPAA does not yet require MFA or segmentation. CMS has not tied payment or Conditions of Participation to the HPH CPGs — they remain voluntary.

Say "proposed." A healthcare CISO knows this material better than we do, and one overstatement ends the meeting.

Risk
The 340B and HASP squeeze lands in FY2027
Name the affected accounts and change the structure

The 340B rebate pilot starts January 1, 2027 and providers project substantial cash-flow drag — at least one system has publicly warned of "diversion of funding for capital." Combined with the 10%/year HASP step-down, this compounds.

Expect DSH-heavy safety-net systems — ECU Health, MUSC, Prisma — to defer discretionary capital. Lead OpEx and as-a-service structures at those accounts specifically rather than applying one commercial model across the vertical.

Risk
We do not know where we stand
Install-base blind spot across the entire vertical

Cisco's network and security incumbency is unconfirmed at every system in this vertical. That is normal — health systems don't publish infrastructure vendors — but it means install-base data has to come from CRM and partner records, not from research.

Do not let anyone treat the absence of evidence as evidence of absence, in either direction. We are as likely to be surprised by a strong position as a weak one.

Risk
Every AI conversation needs a number
CFO review is where these proposals die

Bain and KLAS found the most common formal return threshold among healthcare buyers is 3.0x–3.9x return on original investment. That is the bar, and it is applied.

Presenting AI-enablement infrastructure without a quantified operational return will not survive CFO review regardless of how good the architecture is. If the account team can't state the number, the deal isn't ready to present.
